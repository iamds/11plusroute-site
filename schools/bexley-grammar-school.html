Bexley Grammar School

Welling

September 2027 entry · Last checked 25 November 2026

Your child must be deemed selective in the Bexley Selection Test. Places go first to looked-after children, then up to 12 to Pupil Premium children (nearest first), then to the Bexley-wide top 180 scorers, siblings and staff children. All other places go by straight-line distance; in 2026 the last offer was at 1.847 miles. Test score only matters inside the top 180, or to break exact distance ties.

Who gets a place, in priority order

  1. 1. Looked after / previously looked after children Looked-after and previously looked-after children (including those adopted from state care abroad) who are deemed selective.
  2. 2. Pupil Premium (up to 12 places) Up to 12 places for deemed-selective children eligible for Pupil Premium when you apply, nearest first. Looked-after children are covered by criterion 1 instead.
  3. 3. Guaranteed Grammar School Place (Bexley top 180) The 180 highest scorers across everyone who sat the Bexley Selection Test (for any of the four schools) are 'guaranteed' a place at their preferred Bexley grammar school. Bexley sets the cut-off each year, and it varies with that year's scores.
  4. 4. Siblings Children with a brother or sister (including step/half siblings at the same address) at the school in Years 7-11 when you apply.
  5. 5. Children of staff Children of staff who have worked at the school for at least two years when you apply.
  6. 6. Distance All other deemed-selective applicants, nearest first, measured in a straight line by Bexley's system. About 73% of places went on distance last year.

Good to know

  • There is no catchment area or feeder-school priority: most places go on straight-line distance.
  • About 73% of places went on distance last year, and the last offer in March 2026 was at about 1.8 miles.
  • About 5,900 children sit the Bexley test each year and about 30-35% are deemed selective (5,866 sat in 2025 and 2,070 were selective).
  • If grammar places remain after the first allocation, they go to children one mark below the selective score, then two marks below, and so on.
  • Appeal forms go to the school's Admissions Officer by the end of March.
  • Both papers are sat in one session lasting two and a half to three hours including instructions. All working out goes in the test booklet, as no rough paper is given.
