Queen Elizabeth Grammar School Penrith

Penrith

September 2027 entry · Last checked 25 November 2026

Your child registers online by midnight on 12 July 2026 (end of Year 5) and sits the school's own entrance test, written by FSCE, in September of Year 6: three papers on one day with questions drawn from the whole Key Stage 2 curriculum up to the end of Year 5. Results come by email in mid-October, and only children who reach the required standard can be offered a place. If more qualify than the 160 places, 32 places (20%) go purely by test score. The rest go to looked-after children, then siblings, then up to 10 Pupil Premium children, then staff children, then by straight-line distance: catchment-area children first, then everyone else.

Who gets a place, in priority order

  1. A. Academic ability alone (32 places) The 32 highest-ranked applicants by aggregate entrance test score get a place regardless of where they live.
  2. 1. Looked after / previously looked after children Looked-after and previously looked-after children, including those previously in public authority care outside England.
  3. 2. Sibling at QEGS Children with a brother or sister at the school when they start. If twins or other multiple-birth siblings apply and only some would fit within the 160, all are offered places.
  4. 3. Pupil Premium (up to 10 places) Up to 10 children in receipt of Pupil Premium (including Service Premium). Any of these places not filled by Pupil Premium applicants on National Offer Day go to the next criterion. Pupil Premium children who get a place can receive help with costs such as uniform and trips.
  5. 4. Children of permanent QEGS staff Children of permanent staff with two or more years' service (or recruited for a skill shortage).
  6. 5a. Lives in the designated catchment area, nearest first Remaining places go first to children in the school's designated catchment area, nearest first.
  7. 5b. All other areas, nearest first Then children from everywhere else, nearest first.

Good to know

  • The test covers the whole Key Stage 2 curriculum up to the end of Year 5, including subjects like music, history and science; there is no non-verbal reasoning.
  • There are no past papers and no tutoring is needed: the question style changes every year, and the school's free guide with practice questions is the only official preparation.
  • Your child can sit the test only once.
  • Only 32 places go purely by test score; all other places go to children who passed, by priority group and then distance, not by score.
  • Results arrive in mid-October, before the 31 October council application deadline.
  • Free transport to QEGS is only given if it is your nearest school, and living in the catchment doesn't guarantee school transport.
