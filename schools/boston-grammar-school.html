The Boston Grammar School

Boston

September 2027 entry · Last checked 25 November 2026

Your son must reach the Lincolnshire consortium 11+ qualifying standard (a total of 220 across verbal and non-verbal reasoning). Boys with an EHCP naming the school are admitted first. If more qualified boys apply than there are places, priority goes to looked-after children, then Pupil Premium boys (free school meals in the last six years), then brothers and sisters of current pupils, then distance. So far the school has taken every boy who reached the standard.

Who gets a place, in priority order

  1. 1. Looked after / previously looked after children Looked-after and previously looked-after boys (adopted, or under a child arrangements or special guardianship order, straight from care) who reached the required standard. Includes children who left state care outside England by being adopted.
  2. 2. Pupil Premium (free school meals in the last six years) Qualified boys registered for Pupil Premium, i.e. registered for free school meals at any time in the last six years.
  3. 3. Brother or sister at the school Qualified boys with a brother or sister at the school when he starts. Full brothers and sisters count even if they live in a different household; other children count if they live in the same household for most of term time and an adult there has parental responsibility for them. Twins (or two siblings in the same year group) are considered together when only one place is left, if both reached the standard, and the school may go one over its admission number.
  4. 4. Everyone else by distance All other qualified boys, nearest home first. Distance is a straight line from the Post Office Address Point of the home to that of the school, calculated by Lincolnshire County Council to three decimal places (e.g. 1.543 miles).

  • The school made more offers than its 120 admission number in 2025 (130) and 2026 (136).

  • Boys sit the tests at their primary school or at Boston Grammar School.
  • Appeals must be in by 23 April 2027, and you can appeal whether or not your son reached the standard.
