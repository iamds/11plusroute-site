Reading School

Reading

September 2027 entry · Last checked 25 November 2026

One test of four papers (English, maths, KS2 subjects up to Year 5, and creativity), sat in July at the end of Year 5 by boys living in the catchment area; your son must reach the eligible score in every element. Of the 138 day places, priority goes first to looked-after boys, then to Pupil/Service Premium and social-need boys in the catchment area (all at a score 5 marks lower), staff children, and up to 15 sporting-aptitude places. Then up to 50% of the remaining places go to boys at one of 76 named state primary schools who live in the 13 Reading priority postcodes, up to 80% of what is left to other boys in those postcodes, and the rest to the wider catchment area, all in score order. 12 further weekly-boarding places have their own policy.

Who gets a place, in priority order

  1. 1i. Looked after / previously looked after (incl. adopted from care abroad) Looked-after and previously looked-after boys, including those adopted from state care outside England, who score up to 5 marks below the eligible score. No catchment requirement.
  2. 1ii. Pupil Premium or Service Premium, living in the catchment area Boys entitled to Pupil Premium or Service Premium who live in the catchment area, scoring up to 5 marks below the eligible score.
  3. 1iii. Significant social or welfare need, living in the catchment area Catchment-area boys (living there by 30 June 2026) with a significant social or welfare need to attend this particular school, scoring up to 5 marks below the eligible score.
  4. 1iv. Children of school staff, living in the catchment area Sons of staff (employed 2+ years, or recruited to a skill-shortage post) who live in the catchment area and reach the usual eligible score.
  5. 2. Sporting aptitude (up to 15 places across day and boarding) Boys in the catchment area (or boarding applicants) who reach the eligible score and pass the sporting aptitude threshold, ranked by their aptitude score. Boys who aren't placed here are still considered under the other categories.
  6. 3. Reading feeder primary schools (up to 50% of remaining places) Boys enrolled at one of 76 named state-funded primary schools within 4.6 miles of the Erleigh Road gate (by the July 2026 test day and still at National Offer Day 2027) and whose main home is in a Reading priority postcode. Ranked by test score.
  7. 4. Reading priority postcodes (up to 80% of remaining places) Boys whose main home is in RG1, RG2, RG4, RG5, RG6, RG7, RG8, RG9, RG10, RG30, RG31, RG40 or RG41 on the July 2026 test day and at National Offer Day 2027. Twins/multiples first, then everyone else (including home-schooled boys), by score.
  8. 5. Catchment area (remaining places) Boys whose main home is in the wider catchment area on the July 2026 test day and at National Offer Day 2027. Twins/multiples first, then everyone else, by score.
  9. 6. All other eligible candidates (including outside the catchment area) Everyone else with an eligible score, including boys outside the catchment area, ranked by English, maths and KS2 scores. In a typical year no day places go to boys outside the catchment area.

Good to know

  • The test is sat in July at the end of Year 5, much earlier than most grammar schools. Registration closes in mid-May of Year 5.
  • There's no supplementary information form: the online test registration form collects priority information and evidence.
  • In a typical year no day places go to boys living outside the catchment area.
  • Naming Reading School first on your application is strongly advised. The school doesn't see the order of your preferences.
  • Address checks are strict: a rental taken in the 12 months before the closing date, recent moves or owning more than one property can trigger an investigation, and offers can be withdrawn even after your son has started.
  • There are no past papers, only a familiarisation guide.
