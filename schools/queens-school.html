Queens' School

Bushey

September 2027 entry · Last checked 25 November 2026

A partially selective comprehensive (formerly Bushey Grammar School). Looked-after children, siblings and staff children come first. Next come children living in the priority postcodes around Bushey and Watford. If there are too many of them, up to 35% of places go by rank in the SW Herts Consortium academic test, up to 5% for music and up to 5% for sport, and the rest (at least 10%) go to the nearest. Children outside the priority postcodes come last, by distance. Only priority-postcode children can get test places; others apply under the other rules without a test.

Who gets a place, in priority order

  1. 1. Looked after / previously looked after children Looked-after and previously looked-after children, including those adopted from state care outside England.
  2. 2. Siblings Children with a sibling at the school when you apply. Siblings don't need to sit the tests.
  3. 3. Children of staff Children of staff (SIF needed).
  4. 4. Lives in the priority postcodes All children living in the priority postcodes. In practice this rule is always oversubscribed, so rules 5 and 6 decide who gets in.
  5. 5a. Sport aptitude (up to 5%, priority postcodes only) Up to 13 places for children with a particular aptitude in sport, in score order.
  6. 5b. Music aptitude (up to 5%, priority postcodes only) Up to 13 places for children with a particular aptitude in music, in order of second-round score.
  7. 5c. Academic ability (up to 35%, priority postcodes only) Up to 94 places in strict order of merit on the consortium academic test.
  8. 6. Priority postcodes, nearest first (at least 10% of places) The remaining places (at least 27) go to priority-postcode children living nearest the school.
  9. 7. Outside the priority postcodes, nearest first Children living outside the priority postcodes, nearest first. No places reached this rule in 2025 or 2026.

Good to know

  • The test places are only for children in the priority postcodes, and only apply if the priority postcodes are oversubscribed, which they always have been.
  • In recent years no places have gone to children living outside the priority postcodes.
  • The sports test is very popular: only children with exceptional sporting aptitude living in the priority postcodes have a realistic chance.
  • Siblings don't need to sit any test unless they are applying to another consortium school as a higher preference.
  • Moving shortly after the closing date (for example back to an old home, or after a short let) can lead to an offer being withdrawn.
  • The lowest marks offered in 2026 were 186 (academic), 36 (music) and 84.3 (sport). These are not pass marks.
  • The consortium publishes free familiarisation papers for verbal reasoning and maths, and there is a short break between the two tests.
