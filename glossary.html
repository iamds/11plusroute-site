Glossary

Plain-English meanings of the words used in school admissions. On school pages, words with a dotted underline show these definitions when you hover or tap.

11+
The entrance test for grammar schools, taken at the start of Year 6 (age 10-11). Each area or school sets its own version.
Appeal
If your child isn't offered a place you can appeal to an independent panel. For a grammar school you usually need to show your child is of grammar school ability as well as why the school is right for them.
Aptitude
A natural talent for something, such as music, sport or languages. Some schools give up to 10% of their places to children who show aptitude in a test or audition.
Common Application Form (CAF)
The application you make to your home council (normally by 31 October), listing the schools you want in order of preference. Sitting a test is not an application: you must name the school here too.
EHCP
An Education, Health and Care Plan: a legal document for a child with special educational needs. A school named in a child's EHCP must admit them, before any other priority.
Feeder school
A primary school named by a secondary school in its admission rules; children who attend it get some priority.
Late test
A test sitting for children who missed the main test, for example through illness or moving into the area. Not every area offers one.
Looked-after child
A child in the care of a council. A previously looked-after child was in care and then adopted or placed under a child arrangements or special guardianship order. They get top priority at every state school.
Multiple choice
Questions where the child picks the answer from a set of options.
National Offer Day
1 March, or the next working day: when councils tell families which secondary school they've been offered.
Non-verbal reasoning
Problem solving with shapes and patterns: completing sequences, finding the odd one out and working out codes from diagrams. It involves no reading or maths.
Oversubscription criteria
The rules a school uses to rank applicants when more children apply than there are places. The flow chart on each school page shows them in order.
Partially selective
A state school that isn't a grammar school but gives some of its places by ability or aptitude, and the rest by the usual rules such as distance and siblings.
Pass mark / qualifying score
The score a child must reach to be considered for a grammar school place. Reaching it doesn't guarantee a place if more children qualify than there are places.
Priority area / catchment
The area a school gives priority to. Living inside it improves your chances but doesn't guarantee a place.
Published Admission Number (PAN)
The number of Year 7 places the school offers each year.
Pupil Premium
Extra school funding for children who have had free school meals at any point in the last six years, and for children who are or were in care. Many grammar schools give these children priority or a lower pass mark.
Reallocation
When places given up by other families are re-offered to children on waiting lists, usually from mid-March onwards.
Spatial reasoning
Picturing and moving shapes in your head: rotating, reflecting, folding nets into cubes and fitting pieces together.
Standardised score
A test score adjusted so children of different ages are compared fairly (younger children get a small boost). Scores are usually centred on 100.
Supplementary Information Form (SIF)
A form some schools ask for as well as the council application, usually to register for their test or to claim a priority such as Pupil Premium or faith. Check each school's deadline.
Tie-break
How a school chooses between children with the same priority, usually by distance from home to school, sometimes by random allocation.
Verbal reasoning
Problem solving with words and letters: codes, analogies, word links and letter sequences. It tests reasoning with language rather than knowledge taught in class.
Waiting list
A list of children who weren't offered a place. It's ranked by the school's admission rules, not by when you joined, and schools must keep it until at least 31 December of the year of entry.
Admissions rules change every year. Always check the school's own admissions policy before you apply. Attainment 8: 2026 GCSE results.
