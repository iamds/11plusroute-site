About Quest Assessments

Quest calls its grammar school product Quest Admissions. Each school or consortium has its own test. The school, not Quest, chooses the subjects, the timings and the number of questions.

Quest says its questions use only curriculum content taught up to the end of Year 5.

Quest does not answer questions from parents. Contact the school or council.

What the test is like

  • Grammar school tests are on paper. Children get a question booklet, a separate answer sheet and, for English, a reading booklet.
  • Questions are multiple choice, usually with five options (A to E). Some verbal reasoning questions have six options (A to F) and need two answers.
  • The child draws a line through a box on the answer sheet. A computer reads the sheet, so mistakes must be rubbed out.
  • Wrong answers do not lose marks. Rough working in the booklet is not marked.
  • Some areas add a writing task, which the child writes out in full.

Quest does not release past papers and does not sell practice papers on its own site. Atom Learning sells Quest-style practice papers and mock tests through its paid Atom Home service. Children eligible for pupil premium can get Atom Home free.

Marking and scores

Quest marks the answer sheets centrally and its statisticians age-standardise the scores. Families get a standardised score, not the raw mark. Each area decides how to weight the subjects and what score is needed.

Access arrangements

  • Ask the school or council for access arrangements when you register. Quest does not deal with requests.
  • Arrangements usually match what your child already gets at primary school, such as extra time or enlarged paper. Wirral, for example, can allow 25% extra time or short breaks, but does not give readers or scribes.
