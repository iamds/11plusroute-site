About GL Assessment

GL does not sell one national 11+ test. Each council, consortium or school buys its own version. The local admissions body chooses the subjects and the mix of question types, and can add its own questions. Two GL tests in different areas can look quite different.

GL's four 11+ subjects are verbal reasoning, non-verbal reasoning, English and maths. Some areas also have spatial reasoning questions, usually mixed in with non-verbal reasoning. GL says most tests include verbal reasoning, many include non-verbal reasoning, and fewer include English and maths.

English and maths questions follow the National Curriculum up to the start of Year 6. In Medway the content stops at the end of Year 5.

What the test is like

  • The tests are on paper. Children read the questions in a booklet and mark their answers on a separate answer sheet.
  • Almost all questions are multiple choice. The child draws a pencil line through the box next to the answer. A computer reads the answer sheet, so mistakes must be rubbed out, not crossed out.
  • An invigilator reads out the instructions for each section. Each section starts with an example and practice questions. Only the numbered questions are timed.
  • Calculators are not allowed. In the West Midlands and Trafford, rulers, protractors and scrap paper are not allowed either, and children do rough working in the booklet.
  • Some questions ask for two answers.

Many councils also publish a familiarisation booklet for their own test. Check your area's council or consortium page. GL says local timings and question numbers can differ from its general materials.

GL sells official practice paper packs in verbal reasoning, non-verbal reasoning, English and maths. Each pack has three papers. Lincolnshire tells parents to buy the Multiple Choice packs, not the Standard Format packs.

Marking and scores

Scores are age-standardised to allow for the different ages of children in the year group. Each council or school decides how to weight the subjects and what score is needed.

Access arrangements

  • Ask the school or council for access arrangements when you register. Do not ask GL.
  • GL makes large-print and enlarged papers. There is no braille version.
  • GL guidance allows up to 25% extra time. It says more than that would make the age-standardised score unreliable. Some areas allow less: Buckinghamshire usually gives 10%.
