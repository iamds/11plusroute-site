Quest Assessments 11+ English

Quest English tests reading comprehension, grammar, punctuation, spelling and vocabulary. Children read a passage in a separate reading booklet. Some areas add a writing task.

Schools on this site
12
Format
Paper, multiple choice

Question types

  1. Reading comprehension

    Answer questions on the passage: find facts, work out what a character feels, explain why the writer chose a word or sentence. Some questions ask for two answers.

  2. Spot the mistake

    Find the part of a sentence that has a mistake, or choose 'None'.

    Example A: The dog | B: ran quick | C: across the | D: field. | E: None
    Show answer

    B (quickly).

  3. Punctuation

    Choose where a punctuation mark goes, or choose the sentence that is punctuated correctly.

  4. Complete the sentence

    Choose the word that best completes a sentence.

  5. Replace the words

    Choose the word, such as a pronoun, that can replace the underlined words without changing the meaning.

  6. Synonyms and antonyms

    Choose the word that means the same as, or the opposite of, a given word.

  7. Writing task (some areas)

    Write a piece by hand. This is not multiple choice.

These examples are our own.

How it differs by area

Each area or school has its own version of the test. The same question types can come in a different paper, with a different time and weighting.

How answers are given

  • Grammar school tests are on paper. Children get a question booklet, a separate answer sheet and, for English, a reading booklet.
  • Questions are multiple choice, usually with five options (A to E). Some verbal reasoning questions have six options (A to F) and need two answers.
  • The child draws a line through a box on the answer sheet. A computer reads the sheet, so mistakes must be rubbed out.
