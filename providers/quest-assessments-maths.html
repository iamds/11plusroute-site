Quest Assessments 11+ maths

Quest describes its maths as problem-solving, arithmetic and reasoning. All questions are multiple choice, from options A to E. Calculators are not allowed.

Format
Paper, multiple choice

Question types

  1. Arithmetic

    Work out a calculation or a missing number and choose the answer from A to E.

  2. Multi-step problems

    Problems that need several steps, often about money, time or measures.

  3. Select all that apply

    Choose every correct option. This can mean marking more than one box.

  4. Place value and rounding

    Round a number or work with place value.

  5. Charts and timetables

    Read a bar chart, table or timetable and use the information.

  6. Shape and space

    Angles (for example between clock hands), area of shapes not drawn to scale, and volume of shapes made from cubes.

  7. Missing digits

    Find a missing digit in a written calculation, such as a long multiplication.

Quest's booklets give instructions only, not names for question types, so the names here are ours.

How it differs by area

Each area or school has its own version of the test. The same question types can come in a different paper, with a different time and weighting.

How answers are given

  • Grammar school tests are on paper. Children get a question booklet, a separate answer sheet and, for English, a reading booklet.
  • Questions are multiple choice, usually with five options (A to E). Some verbal reasoning questions have six options (A to F) and need two answers.
  • The child draws a line through a box on the answer sheet. A computer reads the sheet, so mistakes must be rubbed out.

Marking and scores

Quest marks the answer sheets centrally and its statisticians age-standardise the scores. Families get a standardised score, not the raw mark. Each area decides how to weight the subjects and what score is needed.

Access arrangements

  • Ask the school or council for access arrangements when you register. Quest does not deal with requests.
  • Arrangements usually match what your child already gets at primary school, such as extra time or enlarged paper. Wirral, for example, can allow 25% extra time or short breaks, but does not give readers or scribes.
