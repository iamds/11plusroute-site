GL Assessment 11+ English

GL English tests cover reading comprehension, spelling, punctuation and grammar, in line with the National Curriculum up to the start of Year 6. All the questions are multiple choice.

Format
Paper, multiple choice

Question types

  1. Reading comprehension

    Read a passage and answer questions on it: finding facts, working out what the writer means, and the meaning of words. Children can look back at the passage as often as they like.

  2. Spelling

    Each line is split into groups of words. Find the group with a spelling mistake, or choose N if there is no mistake.

    Example The children | were exited | to visit | the zoo. | N
    Show answer

    B (excited).

  3. Punctuation

    The same layout, with one punctuation or capital letter mistake or none.

    Example my brother | and I went | to the park | on Sunday. | N
    Show answer

    A (My).

  4. Choose the best word

    Choose the word or words that complete each line of a passage correctly.

    Example Yesterday we (go, went, going, goes, gone) to the beach.
    Show answer

    went.

These examples are our own. In GL's tests each spelling or punctuation line has at most one mistake. GL suggests about 50 minutes for its free English papers.

How it differs by area

Each area or school has its own version of the test. The same question types can come in a different paper, with a different time and weighting.

BuckinghamshireIn the Verbal Skills paper: an English section of 25 minutes plus 5 minutes' practice, covering reading, grammar, punctuation and spelling.
KentIn a combined English and maths paper: a comprehension and separate literacy questions. There is also a writing task. It is not marked, but a headteacher panel can read it if a case is reviewed.
MedwayA separate English paper: 30 minutes after practice questions, covering comprehension, punctuation and grammar. English counts double.
Birmingham and the West MidlandsA timed comprehension section in each paper.
TraffordComprehension is inside the verbal reasoning section.

How answers are given

  • The tests are on paper. Children read the questions in a booklet and mark their answers on a separate answer sheet.
  • Almost all questions are multiple choice. The child draws a pencil line through the box next to the answer. A computer reads the answer sheet, so mistakes must be rubbed out, not crossed out.
  • An invigilator reads out the instructions for each section. Each section starts with an example and practice questions. Only the numbered questions are timed.

Free practice materials

    Free downloads in verbal reasoning, non-verbal reasoning, English and maths, plus a combined non-verbal reasoning and maths test and a Verbal Skills test. Each set has test booklets, answer sheets and a parent's guide with the answers.

Many councils also publish a familiarisation booklet for their own test. Check your area's council or consortium page. GL says local timings and question numbers can differ from its general materials.

GL sells official practice paper packs in verbal reasoning, non-verbal reasoning, English and maths. Each pack has three papers. Lincolnshire tells parents to buy the Multiple Choice packs, not the Standard Format packs.
